Gold Demand Supported by Continued Central-Bank Buying

Sep 26, 2026 · ASUGALAKX Desk · Commodities Team

Central banks continued to add to their gold reserves for a third consecutive month, extending one of the most persistent official-sector buying streaks in recent memory. The purchases have provided a steady bid beneath the market, offsetting softer jewelry demand in key consuming regions.

The motivations are structural rather than tactical. Reserve diversification away from concentrated currency exposure, a desire for assets without counterparty risk, and the long memory of sanctions-era asset freezes have all pushed official institutions toward the metal. These are slow-moving forces — which is precisely why they matter for the demand outlook.

Physical premiums in Asian markets remain firm versus London spot, suggesting end-user demand is genuine rather than paper-driven. When physical and futures markets tell the same story, the signal is worth more weight than either alone.

What could change the picture

The main risk to the thesis is the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset. If real yields were to rise sharply, the arithmetic for reserve managers would shift at the margin. For now, though, the buying has continued through various rate regimes — evidence that the allocation decision is strategic, not cyclical.

Market Context. Gold's official-sector bid is a flow story with a balance-sheet rationale. It does not set a price target, but it does put a firmer floor under drawdowns than speculative positioning alone could provide.
