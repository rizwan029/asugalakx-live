Web3 Security Basics: Wallet Custody Best Practices

Sep 25, 2026 · ASUGALAKX Academy · Education Team

In Web3, you are your own bank — which means you are also your own security department. Most asset losses in crypto trace back not to broken cryptography but to compromised custody: leaked seed phrases, malicious approvals, and social engineering. The good news is that a short checklist covers the majority of the risk.

Start with the seed phrase. Write it on paper or stamped metal, store copies in separate physical locations, and never photograph it, type it into a website, or share it with anyone — including anyone claiming to be support. No legitimate service will ever ask for it.

Next, separate your wallets by purpose. A hardware wallet for long-term holdings, kept offline except when signing; a smaller hot wallet for daily interaction with applications. If the hot wallet is ever compromised, the damage is contained by design.

Then manage your token approvals. Every time you interact with a decentralized application, you may grant it permission to move your tokens. Review and revoke stale approvals periodically with a reputable approval-management tool — an old unlimited approval is an open door you forgot about.

The human layer

Finally, treat urgency as a red flag. Phishing in Web3 runs on manufactured panic: fake airdrops, impersonated support accounts, “verify now” links. A genuine opportunity will still exist after you pause, verify the source independently, and think. Most losses happen in the five minutes after someone stops thinking.

Market Context. Security is a practice, not a product. No wallet — hardware or otherwise — protects assets from a user who signs a malicious transaction. Slow down at the signing screen: it is the last line of defense, and it is yours.
